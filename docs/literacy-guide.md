# How to verify media before you share it

Deepfakes and edited media spread fastest when people share first and check later. Use this checklist before you forward a video, image, audio clip, or article.

## 1. Pause and look at your reaction

Content built to mislead is designed to trigger anger, fear, or excitement. If a post makes you want to share it immediately, that is a reason to slow down.

## 2. Check the source

- Who posted it first? Open the account and look at its history, not just the post.
- Does a trusted news outlet or official channel carry the same story?
- Is there a named author, a date, and a way to contact the publisher?
- Does the link go where it claims? Expand shortened links and read the full domain.

## 3. Find the original

- Run a reverse image search (Google Lens, TinEye) on the image or on key frames of a video.
- Search for the earliest upload. Old footage is often recycled as new.
- Compare the clip you received with the original: crop, speed, captions, and audio are common edit points.

## 4. Inspect the media itself

**Video**
- Watch the face edges, hairline, teeth, and blinking. Look for flicker or blur that follows the face.
- Check that lip movement matches the audio.
- Look at lighting and shadows. Does the face match the scene?

**Images**
- Check hands, text, jewelry, reflections, and background details, where generated images often fail.
- Look at the file metadata (EXIF) and Content Credentials if present.
- Use error level analysis to look for regions that were pasted or retouched.

**Audio**
- Listen for unnatural pacing, missing breaths, flat emotion, or odd background silence.
- Find the original broadcast or recording and compare.

## 5. Run detection tools, and read results carefully

- Scan videos with Deepware Scanner and run this project's checks.
- A detection score is a probability, not a verdict.
- A clean result does not prove the media is real. Low resolution and heavy compression make detection harder.

## 6. Check with fact-checkers

Search the claim on Google Fact Check Explorer, PIB Fact Check, BOOM, Alt News, Snopes, or Reuters Fact Check.

## 7. Decide

| Situation | What to do |
| --- | --- |
| Source confirmed, other outlets agree | Safe to share, with context |
| Source unclear, tools show no flags | Hold off and keep checking |
| Tools flag manipulation or the source is dubious | Do not share. Report it on the platform |

## Tips for helping others

- Share the correction, not the fake. Do not repost misleading media even to criticize it.
- Teach friends and family to use this checklist.
- If you find a deepfake targeting a person, report it to the platform and, where relevant, to the cyber crime portal at cybercrime.gov.in.
